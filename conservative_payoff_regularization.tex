\begin{abstract}
Discontinuous option payoffs produce smooth prices at every positive time to expiry, while their spatial derivatives concentrate and diverge near the terminal singularities. We give a self-contained one-dimensional analysis connecting this phenomenon to positive diffusion semigroups, weighted spectral theory, and a Gaussian heat parametrix. For smooth uniformly elliptic coefficients we prove the parabolic-scale limit of every digital Greek and construct smooth, local upper and lower payoff approximations. Their prices bracket the original price; their $j$th spatial derivatives are bounded by $C\min(t^{-j/2},\eps^{-j})$; and their price errors are bounded by $C\min(1,\eps/\sqrt t)$ with Gaussian localization. We also prove derivative-error estimates, a two-scale transition limit, local regularization for piecewise smooth continuation values, sharper estimates for piecewise affine payoffs, and propagation through discrete observation dates. A sharp lower bound relates the area of any one-sided digital approximation to its delta cap. The treatment separates whole-line estimates from Dirichlet boundary effects and distinguishes smooth-coefficient results from finite-regularity conclusions. The paper develops a rigorous synthesis and explicit constructions; it does not assert priority over the existing smoothing literature.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\subsection{The market-making problem}
A cash-or-nothing digital put pays $H_K(s)=\1_{\{s<K\}}$. Near expiry and near the strike, a small change in spot can change its value substantially. A dealer can replace the discontinuity by a narrow put spread. The two payoffs
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ramps}
 R^-_\eps(s)=\frac{(K-s)^+-(K-\eps-s)^+}{\eps},\qquad
 R^+_\eps(s)=\frac{(K+\eps-s)^+-(K-s)^+}{\eps}
\end{equation}
satisfy $R^-_\eps\le H_K\le R^+_\eps$, also at $K$ with the convention just fixed. A positive pricing operator preserves these inequalities. This gives a conservative lower price for a bid and upper price for an ask, before transaction costs, funding and inventory considerations. The ramp has slope bounded by $\eps^{-1}$, but corners remain: a ramp stabilizes delta without uniformly stabilizing gamma at expiry. A $C^2$ transition is needed to control both terminal derivatives.

The same mechanism appears at discrete coupon observations and knock-out tests: the continuation value can have a jump when an indicator switches on or off. Continuously monitored barriers are different. They change the evolution operator by imposing killing at a boundary and can create an additional boundary layer. We treat these cases separately.

Risk-neutral valuation begins with a family of expectations. Identifying it as a positive semigroup, and then as the solution operator of a parabolic PDE, adds the tools needed to explain the heuristic: regularity, derivative bounds, weak convergence, localization, and boundary compatibility. The original payoff need not have classical derivatives for its positive-time value to be smooth. Conversely, smoothness at each positive time does not give a derivative bound uniform down to expiry.

In the constant-coefficient heat equation, $DH_K=-\delta_K$ and
\[
 D(e^{taD^2}H_K)(x)=-(4\pi at)^{-1/2}e^{-(x-K)^2/(4at)}.
\]
Thus the delta diverges like $t^{-1/2}$ while converging to a measure in distributions. The $j$th derivative diverges in supremum norm like $t^{-j/2}$. These conclusions follow from an explicit Gaussian, but their nature is local and persists under smooth variable coefficients. Replacing the payoff by a smooth order bracket spreads the singular mass over a window of width $\eps$. The competition is between that window and the diffusion length $\sqrt t$.

\subsection{Results, assumptions, and relation to the references}
The main quantitative theorem is Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. It includes conservative ordering, localized price error, derivative error, and uniform Greek caps. Theorem~\ref{thm:profile} proves the short-time singular profile. Theorems~\ref{thm:general} and \ref{thm:events} treat more general continuation values and repeated observation dates. Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal} gives a sharp constraint on attainable bias under a delta cap.

The organization follows Taylor's route from functional analysis to evolution equations. The weighted Friedrichs realization and spectral scale in Section~\ref{sec:spectral} adapt \cite[Ch.~5, \S1 and Appendix A; Ch.~6, \S1]{TaylorI}. The heat-kernel argument in Section~\ref{sec:kernel} is a one-dimensional, explicitly convergent realization of the parametrix strategy in \cite[Ch.~7, \S13]{TaylorII}. Instead of assuming a differentiated remainder estimate, we prove it using a Gaussian bridge expansion. Evans supplies alternative background on weak solutions, elliptic regularity, and parabolic evolution \cite[\S\S6.2--6.5, 7.1, 7.4]{Evans}; Gilbarg--Trudinger provides the elliptic perspective \cite[Chs.~6, 8]{GT}. The source guide at the end specifies which arguments are reproduced here.

All principal quantitative claims are proved below. The foundational inputs taken as known are the Hilbert-space spectral theorem, the Riesz representation theorem, compactness of the one-dimensional Sobolev embedding, elementary Gaussian integration, and It\^o's formula with existence and uniqueness for globally Lipschitz SDEs. We do not claim a new general heat-kernel theorem. Nor do we claim that these estimates establish optimality of a dealer's full quoting policy. The contribution of this manuscript is the precise combination of these tools with explicit local conservative constructions.

\section{Pricing operators and notation}\label{sec:model}
Time $t\ge0$ always denotes \emph{time to maturity}, so the initial datum of the forward evolution is the financial terminal payoff. On $\R$ put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A}
 A=a(x)D^2+b(x)D-r(x),\qquad
 dX_t=b(X_t)\dd t+\sqrt{2a(X_t)}\dd W_t.
\end{equation}
Except in Section~\ref{sec:finite}, assume
\begin{equation}\label{eq:smooth}
 a,b,r\in C_b^\infty(\R),\qquad 0<\lambda\le a\le\Lambda,\qquad r\ge0.
\end{equation}
Here $C_b^m$ means bounded classical derivatives through order $m$. Constants $C$ may depend on a fixed $T<\infty$, ellipticity, finitely many coefficient norms, derivative orders and a chosen profile, but never on $0<t\le T$ or $0<\eps\le1$. We use
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Pt}
 P_tf(x)=\E_x\!\left[e^{-\int_0^t r(X_s)\dd s}f(X_t)\right].
\end{equation}
For bounded Borel $f$, $P_t$ is positive, $P_{t+s}=P_tP_s$, and $\norm{P_tf}_\infty\le\norm f_\infty$. The semigroup property follows by conditioning at time $t$ and using the Markov property; the other two properties follow directly from the nonnegative discount factor. If $r$ is only bounded below, the same arguments give a factor $e^{t\norm{r^-}_\infty}$.

On an interval $I=(l,h)$ the killed version is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:killed}
 P_t^If(x)=\E_x\!\left[e^{-\int_0^t r(X_s)\dd s}f(X_t)\1_{\{t<\tau_I\}}\right],
 \qquad \tau_I=\inf\{s:X_s\notin I\}.
\end{equation}
It describes zero Dirichlet boundary data. A fixed nonzero rebate can be treated by subtracting a common boundary lifting; it is not part of the homogeneous operator.

The notation $D^jf$ denotes a distributional derivative when $f$ is not smooth. For a piecewise $C^1$ function with finitely many jumps $J_i=f(K_i+)-f(K_i-)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:distjump}
 Df=f'_{\rm reg}+\sum_iJ_i\delta_{K_i}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, integration by parts on each interval yields the interior derivative and the difference of the two traces at each endpoint. Point values at jumps do not alter this distribution, although they matter for pointwise payoff order at time zero.

\section{The weighted spectral foundation}\label{sec:spectral}
In this section $I$ is bounded; coefficients extend smoothly to $\overline I$, $a>0$, and $r\ge0$. We prove the global existence and smoothing mechanism without assuming symmetry in Lebesgue measure.

\subsection{Speed measure and the Friedrichs operator}
Fix $x_0\in I$ and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weight}
 p(x)=\exp\!\left(\int_{x_0}^x\frac{b(s)}{a(s)}\dd s\right),\qquad
 \rho(x)=p(x)/a(x).
\end{equation}
Then $(a\rho)'=b\rho$ and
\[
 A u=\rho^{-1}(p u')'-ru.
\]
The density $\rho$ is a speed density up to normalization. On a bounded interval, $p,\rho$ and their reciprocals are bounded. Set $\mathcal H=L^2(I,\rho\dd x)$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:form}
 \mathfrak q(u,v)=\int_I p u'\overline{v'}\dd x+\int_I r\rho u\overline v\dd x,
 \qquad u,v\in H_0^1(I).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Spectral realization and heat evolution]\label{thm:spectral}
There is a nonnegative self-adjoint operator $L=-A$ on $\mathcal H$, with
\[
 \mathcal D(L)=H^2(I)\cap H_0^1(I),
\]
whose form is \eqref{eq:form}. It has an orthonormal eigenbasis $(e_n)$ and eigenvalues $0<\lambda_n\to\infty$. For $f\in\mathcal H$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spectral}
 e^{-tL}f=\sum_{n=1}^\infty e^{-t\lambda_n}(f,e_n)_\mathcal H e_n
\end{equation}
is the unique weak heat solution in the energy class
$C([0,T];\mathcal H)\cap L^2(0,T;H_0^1)$ with time derivative in $L^2(0,T;H^{-1})$.
Define $\mathcal H_L^s$ by the norm
\[
 \norm f_{\mathcal H_L^s}^2=\sum_n(1+\lambda_n)^s|(f,e_n)_\mathcal H|^2,
\]
using completion for negative $s$. For every $s\in\R$, $\nu\ge0$, and $0<t\le T$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:specsmooth}
 \norm{e^{-tL}f}_{\mathcal H_L^{s+2\nu}}\le C_{\nu,T}t^{-\nu}\norm f_{\mathcal H_L^s}.
\end{equation}
The semigroup is positive and contractive on $L^\infty$, and agrees with $P_t^I$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The form norm $\mathfrak q(u,u)+\norm u_\mathcal H^2$ is equivalent to the $H^1$ norm on $H_0^1$. For $f\in\mathcal H$, Riesz representation gives a unique $u\in H_0^1$ such that
\[
 (u,v)_\mathcal H+\mathfrak q(u,v)=(f,v)_\mathcal H\quad(v\in H_0^1).
\]
The resulting resolvent $R$ is bounded, positive as a Hilbert-space operator, self-adjoint and injective on $\mathcal H$. Its image is dense because $(\operatorname{Ran}R)^\perp=\ker R=0$. The map into $H_0^1$ is bounded, and its inclusion in $\mathcal H$ is compact. The compact self-adjoint spectral theorem gives eigenvectors of $R$ with eigenvalues $\mu_n>0$ tending to zero. Setting $\lambda_n=\mu_n^{-1}-1$ defines $L$ and its spectral domain. Poincar\'e's inequality and $p>0$ imply $\lambda_n>0$.

The weak equation $Lu=f$ says $(pu')'=\rho(ru-f)\in L^2$. Consequently $pu'\in H^1$, hence $u\in H^2$; conversely every $u\in H^2\cap H_0^1$ has the stated form action. This proves the domain identity. Formula~\eqref{eq:spectral} converges in $\mathcal H$ and tends to $f$ as $t\downarrow0$ by dominated convergence. Moreover
\[
 \norm{e^{-tL}f}_\mathcal H^2+2\int_0^t\mathfrak q(e^{-sL}f,e^{-sL}f)\dd s=\norm f_\mathcal H^2.
\]
It follows first for finite sums and then by monotone convergence. The equation gives the required $H^{-1}$ bound for the time derivative. Testing the difference of two energy solutions against itself proves uniqueness (time averaging justifies the test). Finally,
\[
 (1+\lambda)^\nu e^{-t\lambda}\le C_{\nu,T}t^{-\nu}
\]
proves \eqref{eq:specsmooth} term by term.

For positivity, the weak resolvent equation $(1+hL)u=f\ge0$, tested with $u^- =\max(-u,0)$, has nonpositive left-hand side and nonnegative right-hand side, forcing $u^-=0$. If $f\le1$, testing with $(u-1)^+\in H_0^1$ shows $u\le1$, since $r\ge0$. Apply also to $-u$. Thus $(1+hL)^{-1}$ is positive and an $L^\infty$ contraction. Its iterates converge spectrally to $e^{-tL}$, proving the same assertions for the semigroup.

For $f\in C_c^\infty(I)$, elliptic bootstrapping below gives a classical heat solution. Apply It\^o's formula to the discounted $u(t-s,X_s)$ stopped at $\tau_I$ and at $t-\delta$. The drift vanishes; the boundary value is zero. Let $\delta\downarrow0$, using boundedness and continuity at time zero, to obtain \eqref{eq:killed}. Equality extends first in $L^2$ and then to bounded Borel data. One precise extension is to view, at fixed $t>0,x\in I$, both sides as finite measures in the initial datum: they agree on $C_c^\infty(I)$, hence on Borel sets. Point evaluation on the spectral side is continuous after positive time by \eqref{eq:specsmooth} and the elliptic estimate below; positivity supplies the corresponding measure. The stochastic measure has no endpoint mass before exit. This proves the identification.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Elliptic bootstrapping, and why wave flow differs}
\begin{lemma}[One-dimensional elliptic estimate]\label{lem:elliptic}
For every integer $q\ge0$ and smooth $v$ vanishing on $\partial I$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:elliptic}
 \norm v_{H^{q+2}(I)}\le C_q(\norm{Lv}_{H^q(I)}+\norm v_{L^2(I)}).
\end{equation}
In particular, for integer $N\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sobheat}
 \norm{e^{-tL}f}_{H^{2N}}\le C_{N,T}t^{-N}\norm f_\mathcal H.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The identity $a v''=-Lv-bv'+rv$ gives
$\norm{v''}_2\le C(\norm{Lv}_2+\norm{v'}_2+\norm v_2)$.
Integration by parts, using $v=0$ at the endpoints, yields
$\norm{v'}_2^2=-\int v''\overline v\le\norm{v''}_2\norm v_2$.
Young's inequality absorbs the first-derivative term and proves the case $q=0$. If the estimate has been proved through $q-1$, differentiate
$v''=a^{-1}(-Lv-bv'+rv)$ exactly $q$ times. The product rule bounds its $H^q$ norm by $C(\norm{Lv}_{H^q}+\norm v_{H^{q+1}})$. The inductive estimate bounds the latter by $C(\norm{Lv}_{H^{q-1}}+\norm v_2)$; this proves \eqref{eq:elliptic}. Density gives the corresponding weak statements. Iterating it on $v,Lv,\ldots$ gives $\norm v_{H^{2N}}\le C_N\sum_{j=0}^N\norm{L^jv}_2$ for $v\in\mathcal D(L^N)$. The spectral multiplier estimate proves \eqref{eq:sobheat}.
\end{proof}
In one dimension $H^s\hookrightarrow C^m$ when $s>m+\tfrac12$. Thus \eqref{eq:sobheat} gives every positive-time spatial derivative under smooth coefficients. This is exactly the spectral damping mechanism emphasized in Taylor. In contrast, the wave solution has multipliers $\cos(t\sqrt{\lambda_n})$ and $\sin(t\sqrt{\lambda_n})/\sqrt{\lambda_n}$, with no heat-like exponential decay. Its displacement/velocity scale $\mathcal H_L^{s+1}\times\mathcal H_L^s$ is preserved by the invertible wave group; it does not acquire arbitrary regularity as heat flow does.

\begin{remark}[Boundary conditions in fractional domains]
The notation $\mathcal H_L^s$ is intentional: it is not automatically the ordinary $H^s(I)$ with no boundary conditions. For example $\mathcal D(L)=H^2\cap H_0^1$, and for smooth coefficients $\mathcal D(L^N)$ imposes $L^jv|_{\partial I}=0$ for $0\le j<N$. The embeddings used above go from the spectral domain into ordinary Sobolev spaces, not conversely without compatibility. On $\R$, the speed density can grow exponentially, so a bounded digital need not belong to $L^2(\rho\dd x)$. The local and whole-line estimates below therefore do not place the digital in that space.
\end{remark}

\section{A differentiated Gaussian parametrix}\label{sec:kernel}
We now return to \eqref{eq:smooth} on $\R$. This section proves the kernel estimates used later rather than importing them as an unproved assumption.

\subsection{Flattening the principal part}
Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lamperti}
 z=\psi(x)=\int_0^x a(s)^{-1/2}\dd s,\quad x=\chi(z),\quad
 \beta(z)=\left(\frac{b-a'/2}{\sqrt a}\right)(\chi(z)),\quad R(z)=r(\chi(z)).
\end{equation}
Uniform ellipticity makes $\psi$ a bi-Lipschitz diffeomorphism of $\R$. All its positive-order derivatives and those of $\chi$ are bounded. For $u(x)=v(\psi(x))$,
\[
 Au=(v''+\beta v'-Rv)\circ\psi.
\]
Choose $B'=\beta/2$ and put $V=R+\beta'/2+\beta^2/4$. A direct product calculation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gauge}
 (D_z^2+\beta D_z-R)(e^{-B}w)=e^{-B}(D_z^2-V)w.
\end{equation}
The functions $\beta,V$ have bounded derivatives of all orders; $B$ itself may be unbounded, but $|B(w)-B(z)|\le C|w-z|$.

\begin{lemma}[Bridge expansion and differentiated remainder]\label{lem:bridge}
Let $G_t(v)=(4\pi t)^{-1/2}e^{-v^2/(4t)}$ and let $W^0_s=W_s-sW_1$, $0\le s\le1$, be a standard Brownian bridge. The kernel of $D_z^2-V$ is
\begin{align}
 k(t,z,w)&=G_t(z-w)F(t,z,w),\label{eq:bridge}\\
 F(t,z,w)&=\E\exp\!\left[-t\int_0^1 V((1-s)z+sw+\sqrt{2t}W^0_s)\dd s\right].\nonumber
\end{align}
For $0<t\le T$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Fbounds}
 |F-1|\le C_Tt,\qquad |\partial_z^i\partial_w^jF|\le C_{i,j,T}t\quad(i+j\ge1).
\end{equation}
The discounted pricing kernel relative to $\dd y$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:q}
 q(t,x,y)=e^{B(\psi(y))-B(\psi(x))}G_t(\psi(x)-\psi(y))F(t,\psi(x),\psi(y))\psi'(y).
\end{equation}
For all integers $i,j\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gauss}
 |\partial_x^i\partial_y^jq(t,x,y)|\le C_{i,j,T}t^{-(1+i+j)/2}
 \exp\!\left[-\frac{|x-y|^2}{c_{i,j,T}t}\right].
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Iterate Duhamel's equation for $D^2-V$ about the Gaussian semigroup. The term with $n$ insertions of $V$ is an integral over $0<s_1<\cdots<s_n<t$ of a product of $n+1$ Gaussian kernels and $n$ potentials, with sign $(-1)^n$. Integrating the spatial variables in absolute value bounds it by
\[
 G_t(z-w)\frac{(t\norm V_\infty)^n}{n!}.
\]
Thus the series converges absolutely and defines the unique bounded-potential perturbation: uniqueness follows by iterating the difference of two Duhamel solutions, with the same factorial bound. The product of the Gaussian kernels divided by $G_t(z-w)$ is the joint density of a diffusion bridge of variance parameter $2$, at times $s_1,\ldots,s_n$. This follows either by conditioning independent Gaussian increments or by completing squares. Rescale these times by $t$. Symmetry of the cube integral shows that summing the series is precisely the exponential expectation in \eqref{eq:bridge}.

Write its exponent as $-tJ(z,w)$. Every endpoint derivative of $J$ is bounded, because differentiating the affine bridge argument only inserts factors $s$ or $1-s$ and a bounded derivative of $V$. A derivative of $e^{-tJ}$ is a finite sum of products of such derivatives multiplied by $t^\ell e^{-tJ}$, with $\ell\ge1$. Since $|J|\le\norm V_\infty$, dominated differentiation is valid and proves \eqref{eq:Fbounds}. The case $F-1$ follows from $|e^{-tJ}-1|\le t\norm V_\infty e^{t\norm V_\infty}$.

The coordinate and gauge identities give \eqref{eq:q}. This kernel solves $u_t=Au$ with the prescribed initial datum. To identify it with \eqref{eq:Pt}, first take smooth bounded data. Gaussian domination gives a bounded classical solution; It\^o's formula for its discounted backward-time evaluation, stopped in $[-N,N]$ and before time zero, gives the expectation formula as $N\to\infty$ and the remaining time tends to zero. The exit terms vanish because bounded drift and diffusion give vanishing exit probability for fixed initial point as $N\to\infty$. Equality for Borel data follows by equality of the finite kernel measures on smooth compactly supported tests. This also proves positivity and mass at most one, independently of the gauge representation.

Each Gaussian derivative has the bound
$|D^nG_t(v)|\le C_n t^{-(1+n)/2}e^{-v^2/(8t)}$,
by differentiating the Gaussian and absorbing its polynomial into a weaker Gaussian. Differentiate \eqref{eq:q} by the product and chain rules. Derivatives of $F$ are bounded, derivatives of the gauge factor are bounded by $C e^{C|z-w|}$, and coordinate derivatives are bounded. Use
\[
 C|z-w|-\frac{|z-w|^2}{8t}\le C_T-\frac{|z-w|^2}{16t}
\]
and bi-Lipschitz equivalence of $|z-w|$ and $|x-y|$. Terms of lower derivative order can be bounded by the displayed power of $t$ on $0<t\le T$. This proves \eqref{eq:gauss}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Kernel scaling and localization}
For $a_0>0$ let $g_{a_0}(v)=(4\pi a_0)^{-1/2}e^{-v^2/(4a_0)}$. Formula~\eqref{eq:q}, the Taylor expansion of $\psi$, and \eqref{eq:Fbounds} imply, for bounded $\xi,\eta$ and every $m\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rescaledkernel}
 t^{(m+1)/2}\partial_x^mq(t,K+\sqrt t\,\xi,K+\sqrt t\,\eta)
 \longrightarrow \partial_\xi^m g_{a(K)}(\xi-\eta)
\end{equation}
uniformly on compact sets. To see the derivative claim explicitly, the only nonvanishing leading term differentiates the Gaussian $m$ times through $\psi'(x)$; differentiating any coefficient or higher coordinate derivative removes at least one factor $t^{-1/2}$. The gauge factor tends to one and $\psi'(K)=a(K)^{-1/2}$. These observations give exactly the derivative of the Gaussian with variance $2a(K)$.

\begin{lemma}[Localized error bounds]\label{lem:localerror}
Suppose $h$ is supported in an interval $E$ of length $\ell$, $\norm h_\infty\le M$, and $0<t\le T$. Then for every integer $j\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localerror}
 |D^jP_th(x)|\le C_jM t^{-j/2}\min\{1,\ell/\sqrt t\}
 \exp\!\left[-\frac{\dist(x,E)^2}{c_jt}\right].
\end{equation}
For $j=0$ and $h\ge0$, also $P_th\ge0$. If $h_\eps$ is supported in $K+\eps[\alpha,\beta]$, and $h_\eps(K+\eps z)=h_*(z)$ with bounded fixed $h_*$, then at each fixed $t>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:biasexp}
 D^jP_th_\eps(x)=\eps\partial_x^jq(t,x,K)\int_\alpha^\beta h_*(z)\dd z+O(\eps^2),
\end{equation}
locally uniformly in $x$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Integrate \eqref{eq:gauss} against $h$. Split the Gaussian exponent in half and extract $e^{-\dist(x,E)^2/(2ct)}$. The remaining integral is at most both $\ell$ and $C\sqrt t$, yielding \eqref{eq:localerror}. For \eqref{eq:biasexp}, change variables $y=K+\eps z$ and use the mean-value theorem on $\partial_x^jq$ in $y$; its first $y$ derivative is bounded on the fixed compact set involved. The remainder is bounded by $C\eps^2\int|z h_*(z)|\dd z$.
\end{proof}

\section{Singular terminal derivatives and sharp short-time rates}\label{sec:singular}
\begin{theorem}[Digital and jump profiles]\label{thm:profile}
Under \eqref{eq:smooth}, $P_tH_K\to H_K$ in $L^p_{\rm loc}$ for every $1\le p<\infty$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weak}
 D^mP_tH_K\longrightarrow- D^{m-1}\delta_K\quad\hbox{in }\cD'(\R),\qquad m\ge1.
\end{equation}
For each $m\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:profile}
 t^{m/2}D^mP_tH_K(K+\sqrt t\,\xi)
 \longrightarrow -g_{a(K)}^{(m-1)}(\xi)
\end{equation}
locally uniformly in $\xi$. Consequently, for sufficiently small $t$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sharp}
 c_m t^{-m/2}\le\norm{D^mP_tH_K}_\infty\le C_m t^{-m/2}.
\end{equation}
More generally, if bounded $f$ has left and right limits at $K$, with jump $J=f(K+)-f(K-)$, then the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:profile} for $P_tf$ is $Jg_{a(K)}^{(m-1)}(\xi)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $x\ne K$, continuity in probability of $X_t^x$ at $x$ and convergence of the discount to one give $P_tH_K(x)\to H_K(x)$. Dominated convergence on compact intervals gives $L^p_{\rm loc}$ convergence. Testing derivatives against $\varphi\in C_c^\infty$ gives
$\langle D^mP_tH_K,\varphi\rangle=(-1)^m\int P_tH_K D^m\varphi$,
which proves \eqref{eq:weak}.

For the profile, substitute $y=K+\sqrt t\,\eta$ in the differentiated kernel integral over $y<K$. The integrand is the left side of \eqref{eq:rescaledkernel}. For $\xi$ in a compact set, \eqref{eq:gauss} dominates it by $C e^{-(\xi-\eta)^2/c}$ independently of $t$. Dominated convergence, with uniform tail control, yields
\[
 \int_{-\infty}^0\partial_\xi^m g_{a(K)}(\xi-\eta)\dd\eta
 =-g_{a(K)}^{(m-1)}(\xi).
\]
Local uniformity follows from uniform convergence on truncated integration ranges and the uniform Gaussian tail. The upper bound in \eqref{eq:sharp} follows by integrating \eqref{eq:gauss}; for the lower bound choose $\xi$ where $g_{a(K)}^{(m-1)}(\xi)\ne0$.

For the last assertion, $f(K+\sqrt t\,\eta)$ tends to its appropriate one-sided limit for every $\eta\ne0$. Repeat the same dominated-convergence argument. The integral over all $\eta$ of a positive-order Gaussian derivative is zero. The difference between the right and left constants therefore produces $Jg^{(m-1)}_{a(K)}$.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}[What the blow-up statement means]
The lower bound concerns a supremum over a shrinking neighborhood, not every fixed point. For example, in the driftless heat model gamma vanishes exactly at $x=K$, although its supremum grows like $t^{-1}$. A jump of nonzero size forces the rates in \eqref{eq:sharp}; mere distributional convergence alone would not determine them.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Constant-coefficient $L^p$ scaling]\label{prop:lp}
For $A=aD^2+bD-r$ with constant $a>0$, $r\ge0$, and $1\le p\le\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lp}
 \norm{D^mP_tH_K}_{L^p(\R)}=e^{-rt}t^{-m/2+1/(2p)}\norm{g_a^{(m-1)}}_{L^p(\R)},\quad m\ge1,
\end{equation}
where $1/\infty=0$. In particular delta converges weakly to a Dirac mass but has bounded $L^1$ norm.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The solution is $e^{-rt}\int_{-\infty}^K G_{at}(x+bt-y)\dd y$, where $G_{at}(v)=(4\pi at)^{-1/2}e^{-v^2/(4at)}$. Its $m$th derivative is $-e^{-rt}t^{-m/2}g_a^{(m-1)}((x+bt-K)/\sqrt t)$. Change variables in the $L^p$ integral; translation does not affect the norm.
\end{proof}

\section{Smooth conservative digital regularization}\label{sec:regularization}
\subsection{Profiles and deterministic bounds}
Choose $\eta:\R\to[0,1]$ nonincreasing, with $\eta=1$ on $(-\infty,0]$ and $\eta=0$ on $[1,\infty)$. For a $C^m$ profile all derivatives through $m$ must match the constant pieces at both endpoints. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:smoothing}
 H^+_\eps(x)=\eta((x-K)/\eps),\qquad
 H^-_\eps(x)=\eta((x-K+\eps)/\eps).
\end{equation}
Then $H^-_\eps\le H_K\le H^+_\eps$ everywhere. The upper error is supported in $[K,K+\eps]$, the lower error in $[K-\eps,K]$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:payoffder}
 \norm{D^jH^\pm_\eps}_\infty=\eps^{-j}\norm{\eta^{(j)}}_\infty\quad(1\le j\le m).
\end{equation}
In particular the quintic profile
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quintic}
 \eta(z)=1-10z^3+15z^4-6z^5\quad(0\le z\le1)
\end{equation}
with constant extensions is $C^{2,1}$ and satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quinticconstants}
 \norm{\eta'}_\infty=\frac{15}{8},\qquad
 \norm{\eta''}_\infty=\frac{10}{\sqrt3},\qquad \int_0^1\eta(z)\dd z=\frac12.
\end{equation}
Indeed $\eta'=-30z^2(1-z)^2$ and $\eta''=-60z(1-z)(1-2z)$; maximizing these polynomials gives the constants. For unlimited differentiability take
\[
 w(z)=c\exp[-1/(z(1-z))]\1_{(0,1)}(z),\quad \int w=1,\qquad \eta(z)=\int_z^\infty w(v)\dd v.
\]
All derivatives of $w$ vanish at both endpoints, so the extension is $C^\infty$.

\subsection{Propagation of smooth payoff derivatives}
\begin{lemma}[$C_b^m$ stability]\label{lem:Cm}
For $f\in C_b^m(\R)$, $0\le t\le T$, and integers $m\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Cm}
 \norm{P_tf}_{C_b^m}\le C_{m,T}\norm f_{C_b^m}.
\end{equation}
For $j\ge0$ and bounded Borel $f$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Linfsmooth}
 \norm{D^jP_tf}_\infty\le C_{j,T}t^{-j/2}\norm f_\infty\quad(0<t\le T).
\end{equation}
If $D^jf$ is bounded uniformly continuous for $0\le j\le m$, then $P_tf\to f$ in $C_b^m$ as $t\downarrow0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The second bound follows by integration of \eqref{eq:gauss}. For the first, initially take smooth data with bounded derivatives of every order. These data give the classical initial traces of all derivatives: in \eqref{eq:q}, change variables to $w=z+\sqrt t\,v$ before differentiating in $z$. The fixed Gaussian measure in $v$, bounded coefficient derivatives, and \eqref{eq:Fbounds} allow dominated differentiation; every derivative tends uniformly to the corresponding derivative of $f$. Now set $w_j=D^ju$. The product rule gives
\begin{align}\label{eq:triangular}
 (w_j)_t={}&a w_j''+(b+ja')w_j'+c_jw_j+\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}d_{ji}w_i,\\
 c_j={}&\binom j2 a''+jb'-r,\nonumber
\end{align}
where the $d_{ji}$ are bounded combinations of derivatives of $a,b,r$. Terms involving $a$ with derivative order at least three, $b$ with order at least two, and $r$ with order at least one contribute to this last sum. Thus no derivative above $j$ remains in the source. The diffusion with drift $b+ja'$ is nonexplosive. Applying It\^o's formula to this inhomogeneous scalar equation, or the scalar maximum principle with an exponential integrating factor, gives
\[
 \norm{w_j(t)}_\infty\le e^{Ct}\norm{D^jf}_\infty
 +C\int_0^t e^{C(t-s)}\sum_{i<j}\norm{w_i(s)}_\infty\dd s.
\]
Induction on $j$, starting with the contraction for $j=0$, proves \eqref{eq:Cm}.

For general $C_b^m$ data, convolve with smooth approximate identities. The derivatives remain uniformly bounded and converge locally uniformly through order $m$. The kernel estimates give local uniform convergence of all positive-time derivatives; hence the same bounds pass to the limit. For the final assertion, the initial term in the scalar evolution for $w_j$ converges uniformly to $D^jf$: a bounded uniformly continuous function is uniformly approximated by its expectation under a diffusion with bounded coefficients, since $\sup_x\E|X_t^x-x|^2\le Ct$ for small $t$. The bounded zero-order factor tends to one and the source integral is $O(t)$. Induction in \eqref{eq:triangular} completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Conservative price bounds and stabilized Greeks]\label{thm:main}
Assume \eqref{eq:smooth}, let $m\ge2$, and use a $C^m$ profile in \eqref{eq:smoothing}. Write $u=P_tH_K$, $u^\pm_\eps=P_tH^\pm_\eps$. For $0<\eps\le1$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item For all $t\ge0$, $u^-_\eps\le u\le u^+_\eps$.
\item For $0<t\le T$, with $E_+=[K,K+\eps]$ and $E_-=[K-\eps,K]$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mainbias}
 0\le\pm(u^\pm_\eps-u)(t,x)\le C\min\{1,\eps/\sqrt t\}
 e^{-\dist(x,E_\pm)^2/(ct)}.
\end{equation}
For each $j\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:greekerror}
 |D^j(u^\pm_\eps-u)(t,x)|\le C_j t^{-j/2}\min\{1,\eps/\sqrt t\}
 e^{-\dist(x,E_\pm)^2/(c_jt)}.
\end{equation}
\item For $1\le j\le m$ and $0<t\le T$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cap}
 \norm{D^ju^\pm_\eps(t)}_\infty\le C_{j,T,\eta}\min\{t^{-j/2},\eps^{-j}\}.
\end{equation}
In particular the supremum over $0\le t\le T$ is at most $C\eps^{-j}$.
\item As $\eps\downarrow0$, $H^\pm_\eps\to H_K$ in $L^p_{\rm loc}$ for $p<\infty$, and all distributional derivatives converge. For fixed $t>0$ every positive-time derivative converges uniformly on $\R$. For fixed $\eps$, $u^\pm_\eps(t)\to H^\pm_\eps$ in $C_b^m$ as $t\downarrow0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The payoff order and positivity prove assertion 1. The two signed errors are nonnegative, bounded by one, and supported in the specified intervals. Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:localerror} to get assertions 2 and the positive-time convergence in assertion 4. By \eqref{eq:payoffder}, $\norm{H^\pm_\eps}_{C_b^j}\le C_{j,\eta}\eps^{-j}$ when $\eps\le1$. Lemma~\ref{lem:Cm} gives the $\eps^{-j}$ bound; its kernel bound gives the $t^{-j/2}$ bound. Taking the smaller proves assertion 3. The payoff differences have $L^p$ norm at most $\eps^{1/p}$, which implies convergence of distributional derivatives by testing. Finally every derivative through $m$ of the regularized payoff is bounded uniformly continuous, so Lemma~\ref{lem:Cm} proves the fixed-$\eps$ time limit.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Leading bias and the two small parameters]\label{cor:bias}
Let $A_\eta=\int_0^1\eta(z)\dd z$. At fixed $t>0$, locally uniformly in $x$,
\begin{align}
 u^+_\eps-u&=\eps A_\eta q(t,x,K)+O(\eps^2),\label{eq:areas}\\
 u-u^-_\eps&=\eps(1-A_\eta)q(t,x,K)+O(\eps^2).\nonumber
\end{align}
The same expansions hold after any fixed number of $x$ derivatives. If $\eps=\eps(t)=o(\sqrt t)$, then $\norm{u^\pm_{\eps(t)}-u}_\infty\to0$ and
\[
 t^{j/2}\norm{D^j(u^\pm_{\eps(t)}-u)}_\infty\to0.
\]
Absolute convergence of the $j$th derivative error follows, in particular, from $\eps(t)=o(t^{(j+1)/2})$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The upper error profile is $\eta$ on $[0,1]$, the lower is $1-\eta$ on the translated interval. Lemma~\ref{lem:localerror} gives \eqref{eq:areas} and its derivatives. For the joint limits use \eqref{eq:greekerror} with $\eps/\sqrt t\to0$.
\end{proof}
The last condition exposes a limitation: asymptotically negligible relative Greek error does not imply small absolute Greek error near maturity. A fixed positive smoothing width controls Greeks but leaves an order-one price discrepancy at suitably chosen points within its transition as $t\downarrow0$.

\begin{proposition}[Two-scale transition profile]\label{prop:twoscale}
Suppose $\eps(t)/\sqrt t\to\theta\in(0,\infty)$. Then, locally uniformly in $\xi$, for every $j\ge0$,
\begin{align}\label{eq:twoscale}
 t^{j/2}D^jP_tH^+_{\eps(t)}(K+\sqrt t\,\xi)
 &\longrightarrow\int_\R \partial_\xi^jg_{a(K)}(\xi-v)\eta(v/\theta)\dd v,\\
 t^{j/2}D^jP_tH^-_{\eps(t)}(K+\sqrt t\,\xi)
 &\longrightarrow\int_\R \partial_\xi^jg_{a(K)}(\xi-v)\eta(1+v/\theta)\dd v.\nonumber
\end{align}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Change variables $y=K+\sqrt t\,v$ in the kernel formula. The payoff converges pointwise to the indicated profile and remains between zero and one. Equations~\eqref{eq:rescaledkernel} and \eqref{eq:gauss} give compact convergence and uniform Gaussian domination, respectively. Truncation of the $v$ integral proves the result, including local uniformity in $\xi$.
\end{proof}

\section{Black--Scholes and continuous piecewise affine payoffs}\label{sec:BS}
\subsection{Exact digital formulas and spot Greeks}
Under geometric Brownian motion with volatility $\sigma>0$, rate $r\ge0$, and dividend yield $q_d$, set $x=\log s$, $k=\log K$, $a=\sigma^2/2$, and $\mu=r-q_d-\sigma^2/2$. The logarithmic pricing operator is $aD_x^2+\mu D_x-r$. Thus, with $\Phi$ the standard normal distribution function,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BSdigital}
 U(t,x)=e^{-rt}\Phi\!\left(\frac{k-x-\mu t}{\sigma\sqrt t}\right),\qquad
 V(t,s)=U(t,\log s).
\end{equation}
Differentiation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spot}
 \Delta=\frac{U_x}{s},\qquad \Gamma=\frac{U_{xx}-U_x}{s^2},\qquad
 \partial_s^jV=s^{-j}\prod_{\ell=0}^{j-1}(D_x-\ell)U.
\end{equation}
The last identity follows by induction, differentiating $s^{-j}$ and using $s\partial_s=D_x$. On a compact spot interval contained in $(0,\infty)$ the log-derivative bounds therefore imply the corresponding spot bounds, with constants depending on that interval. We do not assert a uniform spot-Greek estimate down to $s=0$ merely from a logarithmic estimate.

A log-window $\eps$ corresponds to an upper spot-window $[K,Ke^\eps]$ and a lower one $[Ke^{-\eps},K]$. If a desk specifies a width directly in spot, apply the profile to $s$ itself. Its logarithmic transform is still smooth and constant outside a compact log-window, so the preceding theory applies; the numerical conversion of derivative caps is made with \eqref{eq:spot}.

For the linear ramp, which is only Lipschitz, convolution nevertheless gives the exact log-delta norm
\begin{equation}\label{eq:erf}
 \norm{D_xP_tR^\pm_\eps}_\infty
 =\frac{e^{-rt}}{\eps}\erf\!\left(\frac{\eps}{4\sqrt{at}}\right).
\end{equation}
Here $R^\pm_\eps$ means the ramp in the $x$ variable. Indeed its derivative is $-\eps^{-1}$ times the indicator of its transition interval, so the convolved derivative is a Gaussian mass over a length-$\eps$ interval. The maximum occurs at its midpoint by symmetry and monotonicity of the Gaussian. Integrating there gives \eqref{eq:erf}. Its limits are $e^{-rt}(4\pi at)^{-1/2}$ when $\eps/\sqrt t\to0$, and $e^{-rt}/\eps$ when that ratio tends to infinity. In contrast, the ramp's second distributional derivative is a signed pair of Dirac masses, so its gamma still diverges as $t\downarrow0$ for fixed $\eps$.

\subsection{Calls, puts, and straddles in an additive model}
The following construction handles continuous kinks and unbounded affine growth without pretending such payoffs are bounded digitals. In this subsection $A=aD^2+bD-r$ has constant coefficients on $\R$; Gaussian moments define $P_tf$ for functions of at most linear growth.

\begin{proposition}[Local smooth bounds for a hinge]\label{prop:hinge}
Let $h(x)=(x-K)^+$ and let $\zeta\in C_c^\infty((-1,1))$ be nonnegative, even, of integral one. Put $\zeta_\eps(y)=\eps^{-1}\zeta(y/\eps)$ and
$h^+_\eps=\zeta_\eps*h$.
Choose $\vartheta\in C_c^\infty((-2,2))$, $0\le\vartheta\le1$, with $\vartheta=1$ on $[-1,1]$, and set
\[
 h^-_\eps=h^+_\eps-c_\zeta\eps\vartheta((x-K)/\eps),\qquad c_\zeta=\int|z|\zeta(z)\dd z.
\]
Then $h^-_\eps\le h\le h^+_\eps$, both regularizations agree with $h$ for $|x-K|\ge2\eps$, and for $j\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hingecaps}
 \norm{D^jh^\pm_\eps}_\infty\le C_j\eps^{1-j}\quad(0<\eps\le1).
\end{equation}
For $0<t\le T$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hingebias}
 0\le\pm(P_th^\pm_\eps-P_th)\le C\eps\min\{1,\eps/\sqrt t\},
\end{equation}
and, for $j\ge2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hingegreek}
 \norm{D^jP_th^\pm_\eps}_\infty\le C_j\min\{t^{-(j-1)/2},\eps^{1-j}\}.
\end{equation}
These statements extend by signed linear combinations to every continuous piecewise affine payoff with finitely many knots, choosing upper or lower components according to the coefficient signs.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Convexity and the zero first moment of $\zeta$ give $h^+_\eps\ge h$. Since $h$ is 1-Lipschitz, $0\le h^+_\eps-h\le c_\zeta\eps$. If $|x-K|\ge\eps$, convolution sees a single affine branch and equals $h$ exactly. On $|x-K|\le\eps$ the subtracted buffer dominates the convolution error; outside that interval the error is zero and the buffer is nonnegative. Hence $h^-_\eps\le h$. Its support and smoothness properties follow from those of the cutoff.

We have $(h^+_\eps)'=\zeta_\eps*\1_{(K,\infty)}$ and $(h^+_\eps)''=\zeta_\eps(\cdot-K)$. Differentiation and the buffer scaling give \eqref{eq:hingecaps}. Each price error has amplitude at most $C\eps$ and support of length at most $4\eps$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:localerror}, valid here by the explicit constant kernel, gives \eqref{eq:hingebias}. Derivatives commute with the constant-coefficient semigroup. The terminal second derivatives have uniformly bounded $L^1$ norm, including the buffer because its second derivative has size $O(\eps^{-1})$ on length $O(\eps)$. Thus for $j\ge2$,
\[
 \norm{D^jP_th^\pm_\eps}_\infty
 \le e^{-rt}\norm{D^{j-2}G_{at}}_\infty\norm{D^2h^\pm_\eps}_{L^1}
 \le C_j t^{-(j-1)/2}.
\]
Convolving the bounded terminal $j$th derivative gives the alternative bound $C_j\eps^{1-j}$. Finally any continuous piecewise affine function is an affine function plus a finite sum of signed hinges. Positive coefficients use the corresponding upper/lower hinge, and negative coefficients reverse them. Linearity and positivity finish the proof.
\end{proof}
A put is $h(x)-(x-K)$ and a straddle is $2h(x)-(x-K)$. Hence their regularity and bias orders follow immediately. The $O(\eps^2/\sqrt t)$ small-window bias of a continuous kink is one order better than the digital's $O(\eps/\sqrt t)$ bias. For geometric Black--Scholes, one can use the same spot-payoff construction and the positive lognormal kernel; on a compact neighborhood of a positive strike the localized price-error argument again yields this bias order. Formula~\eqref{eq:hingegreek} as stated is the additive constant-coefficient result, not an assertion that spot derivatives commute with the Black--Scholes operator.

\section{Local conservative smoothing of general continuation values}\label{sec:general}
A jump subtraction $f=g+J\1_{(K,\infty)}$ does not by itself make $g$ smooth: derivative mismatches remain. The following construction avoids that gap.

\begin{theorem}[Local envelope construction]\label{thm:general}
Let $\Sigma=\{K_1,\ldots,K_N\}$ be finite. Suppose $f$ is bounded, smooth on each component of $\R\setminus\Sigma$, and all one-sided derivatives of every order are bounded up to the endpoints. Assign each $f(K_i)$ between its two one-sided limits. For sufficiently small $\eps>0$ there exist $S^-_\eps f,S^+_\eps f\in C_b^\infty$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:generalorder}
 S^-_\eps f\le f\le S^+_\eps f,\qquad S^\pm_\eps f=f\quad\text{if }\dist(x,\Sigma)\ge3\eps.
\end{equation}
For every $j\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:generalcap}
 \norm{D^jS^\pm_\eps f}_\infty\le C_{f,j}\eps^{-j},\qquad
 \norm{S^\pm_\eps f-f}_{L^p}\le C_f\eps^{1/p}\quad(1\le p<\infty).
\end{equation}
Under \eqref{eq:smooth} their prices preserve the bracket, satisfy the derivative cap $C\min(t^{-j/2},\eps^{-j})$, and have error bounded by the sum of $N$ terms of the form \eqref{eq:mainbias} with windows $[K_i-3\eps,K_i+3\eps]$.
If $f$ is continuous and globally Lipschitz, the payoff error is $O(\eps)$ in supremum norm and the price error is $O(\eps\min(1,\eps/\sqrt t))$. For this continuous case and $j\ge1$, the terminal derivative bound improves to $C_{f,j}\eps^{1-j}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose a nonnegative smooth mollifier $\zeta_\eps$ supported in $[-\eps,\eps]$, of integral one, and define the measurable envelopes
\[
 M_\eps f(x)=\sup_{|z-x|\le2\eps}f(z),\qquad
 m_\eps f(x)=\inf_{|z-x|\le2\eps}f(z).
\]
For the present finite-piece class these functions are measurable (the supremum can be computed from a countable dense subset on each smooth branch and the finitely many jump values). Put
$U_\eps=\zeta_\eps*M_\eps f$ and $L_\eps=\zeta_\eps*m_\eps f$.
For $|y-x|\le\eps$, the interval centered at $y$ of radius $2\eps$ contains $x$. Hence $M_\eps f(y)\ge f(x)$ and $m_\eps f(y)\le f(x)$, and integration proves
$L_\eps(x)\le f(x)\le U_\eps(x)$ pointwise.

Take disjoint windows of radius $3\eps$ about the $K_i$ and a smooth $0\le\theta_\eps\le1$ which equals one when $\dist(x,\Sigma)\le\eps$ and zero when the distance is at least $3\eps$, with $\norm{D^j\theta_\eps}_\infty\le C_j\eps^{-j}$. Define
\[
 S^+_\eps f=\theta_\eps U_\eps+(1-\theta_\eps)f,\qquad
 S^-_\eps f=\theta_\eps L_\eps+(1-\theta_\eps)f.
\]
The nonsmooth part of $f$ is multiplied by a function identically zero in a neighborhood of every jump. Therefore these are smooth; the convex combination preserves order and gives exact locality. Differentiating the mollifier gives $\norm{D^jU_\eps}_\infty+\norm{D^jL_\eps}_\infty\le C_j\eps^{-j}\norm f_\infty$. The product rule, bounded branch derivatives, and the cutoff estimates prove \eqref{eq:generalcap}. The error has bounded amplitude and support of total length at most $6N\eps$, giving the $L^p$ estimate. Apply positivity, Lemma~\ref{lem:Cm}, and Lemma~\ref{lem:localerror} to obtain all asserted price estimates.

If $f$ is globally Lipschitz with constant $L_f$, every point used in the envelope-convolution lies within $3\eps$ of $x$. Consequently $|U_\eps(x)-f(x)|$ and $|L_\eps(x)-f(x)|$ are at most $3L_f\eps$. Both envelopes are $L_f$-Lipschitz: translate the intervals in their definition and use the Lipschitz bound on $f$. For $j\ge1$, cancellation of $D^j\zeta_\eps$ therefore gives a bound $C_jL_f\eps^{1-j}$ for their mollifications. On the cutoff annuli, differentiating $S^+_\eps f=f+\theta_\eps(U_\eps-f)$ uses the $O(\eps)$ bound for the undifferentiated difference and bounded branch derivatives for the other factors. Every term is bounded by $C_{f,j}\eps^{1-j}$; the lower case is identical. The localized error estimate with amplitude $O(\eps)$ gives the improved price bound.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
These operators are nonlinear, and their purpose is order and locality rather than linearity or preservation of convexity. They match every derivative of the original branches outside the windows without an unspecified jet-interpolation correction. The theorem does not claim that higher Greeks keep their signs: price positivity does not imply derivative-order preservation.
\end{remark}

\section{Repeated observation dates and killed evolution}\label{sec:events}
\subsection{Monotone observation maps}
Let $P_i$ be positive contractions (whole-line or killed pricing semigroups over intervals $\Delta_i>0$). At observation date $i$ let
\[
 F_i(v)(x)=c_i(x)+d_i(x)v(x),\qquad 0\le d_i\le1,
\]
where $c_i,d_i$ are bounded and may have finitely many jumps. This covers a conditional coupon and survival or knock-out indicator, with any fixed rebate absorbed into $c_i$. The exact backward recursion is
$v_i=P_iF_i(v_{i+1})$ for $i=n-1,\ldots,0$, with terminal datum $v_n$.

\begin{theorem}[Conservative recursion and error propagation]\label{thm:events}
Start from $v_n^-\le v_n\le v_n^+$. At each step define
\[
 v_i^+=P_iS_i^+F_i(v_{i+1}^+),\qquad
 v_i^-=P_iS_i^-F_i(v_{i+1}^-),
\]
where $S_i^-g\le g\le S_i^+g$ are order brackets for the actual input $g$ at that step. Then $v_i^-\le v_i\le v_i^+$ for every $i$. If
\[
 h_i^+=S_i^+F_i(v_{i+1}^+)-F_i(v_{i+1}^+),\qquad
 h_i^-=F_i(v_{i+1}^-)-S_i^-F_i(v_{i+1}^-),
\]
then both are nonnegative and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:recerror}
 \norm{v_i^\pm-v_i}_\infty\le\norm{v_n^\pm-v_n}_\infty+
 \sum_{j=i}^{n-1}\norm{P_jh_j^\pm}_\infty.
\end{equation}
For whole-line diffusions satisfying \eqref{eq:smooth}, if each residual has bounded amplitude $M_j$ and support in $N_j$ windows of width $O(\eps_j)$, its summand is at most
$C_jN_jM_j\min(1,\eps_j/\sqrt{\Delta_j})$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Multiplication by $d_i\ge0$, addition of the same $c_i$, order bracketing, and positivity of $P_i$ preserve inequalities; backward induction proves the bracket. For the upper error there is the exact identity
\[
 v_i^+-v_i=P_i\{d_i(v_{i+1}^+-v_{i+1})+h_i^+\}.
\]
The lower error has the same identity with $v_i-v_i^-$ and $h_i^-$. Since $0\le d_i\le1$ and $P_i$ is contractive, iteration gives \eqref{eq:recerror}. The final estimate is Lemma~\ref{lem:localerror} summed over the windows. No monotonicity of $S_i$ as an operator on different inputs is needed; its bracket property at each actual input suffices.
\end{proof}
With positive inter-observation times and smooth branch functions, the inputs to each new observation are piecewise smooth, so Theorem~\ref{thm:general} applies. Constants must be recalculated or bounded for these inputs; the theorem does not assume they remain independent of the number or spacing of observations. Between dates, Theorem~\ref{thm:main} or Lemma~\ref{lem:Cm} controls the newly regularized value. Observation indicators can recreate a jump, so regularity is restarted at each observation.

\subsection{Dirichlet boundaries: what transfers and what does not}
\begin{proposition}[Killed price brackets and a boundary obstruction]\label{prop:boundary}
Let $I$ be bounded and assume the coefficients extend to \eqref{eq:smooth} on $\R$. If $f^-\le f\le f^+$ on $I$, then $P_t^If^-\le P_t^If\le P_t^If^+$. If a signed payoff error is nonnegative and supported in interior windows, its killed price is bounded by the whole-line localized price bound after extending that error by zero.
However, smoothness of $f$ on $\overline I$ alone does not imply a derivative bound uniform as $t\downarrow0$ for $P_t^If$. Such bounds require boundary compatibility or restriction to an interior region.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The bracket follows from \eqref{eq:killed}. For $h\ge0$, extend by zero outside $I$; dropping the survival indicator gives $0\le P_t^Ih\le P_th$, proving the price bound. This argument does not compare derivatives.

For the obstruction take the heat operator $D^2$ on $(0,\infty)$, killed at zero, with $f\equiv1$. The reflection kernel is $G_t(x-y)-G_t(x+y)$ for $x,y>0$. Integrating in $y$ yields
\[
 P_t^{(0,\infty)}1(x)=\erf(x/(2\sqrt t)),\qquad
 D P_t^{(0,\infty)}1(0+)=\frac1{\sqrt{\pi t}}.
\]
Thus even constant smooth data create a boundary delta layer when they fail the Dirichlet trace condition. The same phenomenon occurs on a bounded interval $(0,L)$: odd periodic extension of $1$ has jumps at $0$ and $L$, and Gaussian convolution gives a derivative at zero equal to $1/\sqrt{\pi t}+O(t^{-1/2}e^{-L^2/(ct)})$. This follows directly by summing the reflected Gaussians; all image points other than zero have distance at least $L$. Hence the obstruction also holds in the bounded spectral setting.
\end{proof}
This distinction is also central to Taylor's treatment of boundary layers \cite[Ch.~6, \S8]{TaylorI}. For smooth compatible data one useful sufficient statement follows from Section~\ref{sec:spectral}: if $f\in\mathcal D(L^N)$, $2N>j+\tfrac12$, then
\[
 \sup_{t\ge0}\norm{D^je^{-tL}f}_\infty\le C\norm f_{\mathcal H_L^{2N}}.
\]
Indeed the semigroup contracts that spectral norm and elliptic embedding applies. This bound makes the required compatibility explicit. It is not the same as a bound by an arbitrary $C^j$ terminal norm. Interior singular profiles and boundary profiles should not be identified without checking the geometry and monitoring rule.

\section{Finite coefficient regularity}\label{sec:finite}
The full derivative hierarchy above deliberately uses smooth coefficients. We give two precise finite-regularity statements instead of an unspecified assertion that the same estimates hold for every Greek under Lipschitz coefficients.

\begin{proposition}[Finite-regularity versions]\label{prop:finite}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}
\item On a bounded interval, let $a,b,r\in C^{k,1}(\overline I)$, $k\ge0$, with $a\ge\lambda>0$, $r\ge0$. The form construction, domain identity, spectral-scale estimate, positivity, and weak uniqueness in Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral} remain valid. For every $f\in L^2(I,\rho\dd x)$ and $t>0$, $e^{-tL}f\in C^{k+2,1}(\overline I)$. This is a one-dimensional conclusion, not a general-dimensional endpoint Schauder assertion.
\item Fix an integer $m\ge2$. On $\R$, it suffices to assume bounded uniformly elliptic $a,b,r\in C_b^{m+3}$, $r\ge0$, for Theorem~\ref{thm:profile} through derivative order $m$ and Theorem~\ref{thm:main} through order $m$ (including the derivative-error estimates through $m$). The constants depend on finitely many of these norms. In particular $C_b^{m+3,1}$ is a sufficient, nonminimal finite-regularity hypothesis.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For assertion 1, the weights in \eqref{eq:weight} are positive Lipschitz functions, bounded above and below. The form and compact-resolvent arguments are unchanged. From $(pu')'\in L^2$ and Lipschitz $p^{-1}$ we again get $u\in H^2$, proving the domain identity. Spectral damping gives $u_j=L^je^{-tL}f\in\mathcal D(L)$ for all $j$. Hence every $u_j$ is $H^2$, in particular $C^1$ in one dimension. The differential identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finiteode}
 u_j''=a^{-1}(-u_{j+1}-bu_j'+ru_j)
\end{equation}
has a continuous right-hand side, so each $u_j$ is $C^2$. On the compact interval $u_j,u_j'$ are now Lipschitz, and the coefficients and $u_{j+1}$ are Lipschitz. Thus \eqref{eq:finiteode} is Lipschitz, giving $u_j\in C^{2,1}$ for every $j$. Inductively, if all $u_j\in C^{\ell+1,1}$ and the coefficients are $C^{\ell,1}$, the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:finiteode} is $C^{\ell,1}$ and therefore $u_j\in C^{\ell+2,1}$. Induction for $\ell\le k$ proves the claim. All statements concern fixed positive $t$; their norms need not stay bounded at $t=0$.

For assertion 2, the flattening and gauge formulas use one derivative of $a$ in $\beta$ and one additional derivative in $V$. Derivatives of $V$ through $m+1$ are bounded under the stated assumption. The bridge proof therefore supplies kernel derivatives through total order $m+1$, sufficient for all asserted order-$m$ estimates and the first terminal-space derivative used in the bias expansions. The triangular equations in Lemma~\ref{lem:Cm} through $m$ require no more derivatives. The proofs of the profile and regularization theorems can consequently be read verbatim up to order $m$. Smooth approximation, with common coefficient bounds and ellipticity, removes any unused higher smoothness in the intermediate classical calculations; alternatively the bridge construction itself already has the required finite differentiability.
\end{proof}
For $k=0$, positive-time $C^{2,1}$ in the bounded one-dimensional problem does not provide the entire hierarchy of sharp Gaussian derivative estimates, nor does it imply a maturity-uniform gamma cap for arbitrary boundary data. These are distinct assertions. In several dimensions there is also no universal speed density making an arbitrary drift symmetric: an integrability condition is needed. No multidimensional version is claimed here.

\section{Bias versus derivative caps}\label{sec:optimization}
The construction makes the price cost explicit. For a fixed profile,
\[
 \text{upper bias}\sim\eps A_\eta q(t,x,K),\qquad
 \norm{D^jH^+_\eps}_\infty=\eps^{-j}\norm{\eta^{(j)}}_\infty.
\]
The next bound shows that a first-derivative cap forces a nonzero error area, independently of the chosen profile.

\begin{proposition}[Sharp area bound under a delta cap]\label{prop:optimal}
Let $v:\R\to[0,1]$ be Lipschitz, $v(x)=1$ for $x\le K$, $v(x)\to0$ as $x\to\infty$, and $\Lip(v)\le L$ with $L>0$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:arealower}
 \int_K^\infty v(x)\dd x\ge\frac1{2L}.
\end{equation}
Equality holds for the ramp $v(x)=(1-L(x-K))^+$ on $x\ge K$. Among $C^1$ functions with the same left constant branch, equality is impossible, but the infimum remains $1/(2L)$, even when restricting to $C^\infty$ nonincreasing profiles eventually equal to zero.
For the positive pricing kernel,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pricelower}
 P_t(v-H_K)(x)\ge\int_0^{1/L}q(t,x,K+s)(1-Ls)\dd s.
\end{equation}
At fixed $t>0$, as $L\to\infty$, the right-hand side is
$q(t,x,K)/(2L)+O_{t,x}(L^{-2})$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Since $v(K)=1$, Lipschitz continuity implies $v(K+s)\ge\max(1-Ls,0)$. Integrating gives \eqref{eq:arealower}. Equality of the integrals of these nonnegative functions forces equality everywhere by continuity, hence the ramp, which is not $C^1$ at $K$ or $K+1/L$.

For smooth approximations, choose a smooth function $w_\delta$ supported in $(0,1/L+2\delta)$, with $0\le w_\delta\le L$, integral one, and tending in $L^1$ to $L\1_{(0,1/L)}$ as $\delta\downarrow0$. Such a function is obtained by convolving $L\1_{(\delta,\delta+1/L)}$ with a nonnegative smooth kernel of radius less than $\delta$. Define $v_\delta(K+s)=\int_s^\infty w_\delta(y)\dd y$ for $s\ge0$, with the left constant branch. It is smooth, nonincreasing, has derivative bound $L$, and its area equals $\int_0^\infty y w_\delta(y)\dd y\to1/(2L)$. This proves the infimum statement. Multiply the pointwise ramp lower bound by $q\ge0$ and integrate to obtain \eqref{eq:pricelower}. Taylor expansion in $s$ gives the asymptotic, since $\int_0^{1/L}(1-Ls)\dd s=1/(2L)$ and $\int_0^{1/L}s(1-Ls)\dd s=1/(6L^2)$.
\end{proof}
For the quintic, a terminal delta cap $D_*$ and gamma cap $G_*$ are satisfied whenever
\begin{equation}\label{eq:widthchoice}
 \eps\ge\max\left\{\frac{15}{8D_*},\sqrt{\frac{10}{\sqrt3\,G_*}}\right\}.
\end{equation}
This is an exact terminal statement in the chosen coordinate. In a constant-coefficient model, convolution preserves these caps (with discount factor at most one). For variable coefficients the sufficient finite-horizon caps include the propagation constants of Lemma~\ref{lem:Cm}; for spot Greeks use \eqref{eq:spot}. Choosing a feasible width by \eqref{eq:widthchoice} is not an optimization theorem under simultaneous delta and gamma constraints. Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal} solves only the stated delta-area problem.

\section{Discussion and precise scope}
The results separate three operations. Positivity transfers a terminal order bracket to a price bracket. Heat regularity explains the positive-time smoothing of a singular payoff and the blow-up needed to recover its distributional derivatives. A deterministic local smoothing then replaces that singular terminal structure by controlled classical derivatives. Neither positivity nor regularity implies preservation of every Greek's sign.

The sharp rates $t^{-j/2}$ for a digital and $t^{-(j-1)/2}$ for a continuous affine kink reflect whether the singular measure first appears in the first or the second terminal derivative. For a digital, the dimensionless ratio $\eps/\sqrt t$ controls both price bias and the transition from heat-scale to payoff-scale Greeks. The two-scale theorem describes the intermediate regime, while the area lower bound shows why stability cannot be obtained at zero conservative price cost under a finite delta cap.

For discrete contractual events the same order argument iterates, with explicit accumulation of local errors. For continuous killing the price argument remains valid, but boundary derivative layers require compatibility. Finally, low coefficient regularity still permits a rigorous spectral construction and one-dimensional elliptic bootstrap, while sharp high-order Gaussian estimates require additional derivatives. These distinctions are necessary parts of the mathematical statement, rather than technical exceptions to a universal smoothing claim.
