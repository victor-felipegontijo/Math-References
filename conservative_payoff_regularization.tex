\section{The multidimensional diffusion and its pricing semigroup}
\label{sec:diffusion}
\noindent\textbf{Aim of this section.}
In log-spot coordinates, the Black--Scholes model is the constant-coefficient diffusion
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BS}
  \dd X_t=\left(r-q_d-\frac{\sigma^2}{2}\right)\dd t+\sigma\dd W_t.
\end{equation}
Its explicit Gaussian kernel explains why pricing and smoothing can be studied by direct differentiation. The goal here is to retain the \emph{probabilistically selected} price for variable-coefficient, multidimensional models and then justify the analytic objects needed for interior parabolic estimates. We distinguish four steps:
\begin{equation*}
\boxed{\begin{gathered}
 \text{weighted pricing expectations}\ \Longrightarrow\
 \text{strongly continuous realizations}\\[2pt]
 \Downarrow\\[-1pt]
 \text{closed differential generator}\ \Longrightarrow\
 \text{local energy solution and strong trace}.
\end{gathered}}
\end{equation*}
The final arrow is proved, rather than inferred from It\^o's formula. See Ethier--Kurtz \cite[Chs.~1, 4]{ethier-kurtz} for the semigroup/martingale perspective, Ouhabaz \cite[Chs.~1, 4, 6]{ouhabaz} for form realizations and kernels, and Evans \cite[\S 7.1]{evans} for parabolic energy solutions.

\subsection{Standing whole-space model and algebraic pricing maps}
\label{subsec:model}
Fix a state dimension $d\ge1$, Brownian dimension $m\ge d$, and a finite horizon $T>0$. Under a \emph{specified} pricing measure, let $W$ be an $m$-dimensional Brownian motion and consider
\begin{equation}\label{eq:SDE}
 \dd X_t^x=b(X_t^x)\dd t+\sigma(X_t^x)\dd W_t,\qquad X_0^x=x\in\Rd.
\end{equation}
Write $b:\Rd\to\Rd$, $\sigma:\Rd\to\R^{d\times m}$, and $r:\Rd\to\R$ for the drift, volatility, and short rate. \emph{For the results proved in this section} we impose the base assumptions below; higher regularity, when needed later, can be added without changing the probability--energy argument.

\begin{definition}[Standing assumptions $(\mathrm H_0)$]\label{def:H0}
The coefficients $b,\sigma,r$ are bounded and globally Lipschitz, $r\ge0$, and the symmetric matrix
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a}
 a(x)=\frac12\sigma(x)\sigma(x)^\top,\qquad
 a^{ij}(x)=\frac12\sum_{\ell=1}^m\sigma^{i\ell}(x)\sigma^{j\ell}(x),
\end{equation}
satisfies, for constants $0<\lambda\le\Lambda<\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ellip}
 \lambda|\xi|^2\le\xi^\top a(x)\xi\le\Lambda|\xi|^2
 \qquad (x,\xi\in\Rd).
\end{equation}
For subsequent high-order statements one may strengthen $(\mathrm H_0)$ to $b,\sigma,r\in C_b^{k,1}$ with $k\ge1$. No such strengthening is used in the bridge theorem below.
\end{definition}

The globally Lipschitz coefficients ensure a unique nonexplosive strong solution for every $x$. Ellipticity is a separate analytic hypothesis: it controls all state directions. Because $a$ has full rank, the assumed Brownian dimension necessarily satisfies $m\ge d$. Conversely, a bounded Lipschitz uniformly positive definite $a$ can be modeled with $m=d$ and $\sigma=(2a)^{1/2}$, since the matrix square-root map is Lipschitz on uniformly positive definite matrices with bounded spectrum.

For bounded Borel $f$ define the \emph{discounted pricing family}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:P}
 (P_tf)(x)=\E_x\!\left[
   \exp\left(-\int_0^t r(X_s)\dd s\right)f(X_t)
 \right],\qquad 0\le t\le T.
\end{equation}
The Markov property and multiplicativity of the discount factor give $P_{t+s}=P_tP_s$ whenever $t+s\le T$. Positivity and $r\ge0$ imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:submarkov}
 P_t f\ge0\ \text{if }f\ge0,\qquad
 P_t\mathbf1\le\mathbf1,\qquad
 \norm{P_tf}_\infty\le\norm f_\infty.
\end{equation}
Thus $(P_t)$ is initially a \emph{sub-Markov algebraic semigroup} on bounded Borel functions. It need not be conservative ($P_t\mathbf1=1$) because of discounting. The chosen measure does not, by itself, establish market completeness, its uniqueness, or replication.

\begin{remark}[State coordinate and time convention]
Constant nonsingular covariance in a vector of log-prices recovers correlated multidimensional Black--Scholes. The general coefficients in $(\mathrm H_0)$ may depend on the state. Here $t$ is \emph{time elapsed since payoff evaluation}: a financial terminal payoff therefore appears as the initial datum $u(0)=f$ of the backward \emph{state-variable} equation.
\end{remark}

\subsection{From pricing expectations to a local parabolic energy solution}
\label{subsec:prob-analytic}
The four subsections below make the successive analytic structures explicit: a weighted operator bound, a strongly continuous realization, a closed generator with a precise domain, and finally a weak PDE realization for the original payoff class.
\subsubsection{Step I: kernels, exponential payoffs, and the scope of the semigroup}
\label{subsec:weights}
Uniform ellipticity and bounded Lipschitz coefficients yield a transition density $p(t,x,y)$ satisfying a finite-horizon Aronson upper bound; see \cite{aronson1967,aronson1968} and the parabolic kernel discussion in \cite[Ch.~6]{ouhabaz}. The discounted measure has a density $q(t,x,y)$ such that $0\le q\le p$ (disintegrate the discount factor conditional on $X_t=y$). Consequently, for $0<t\le T$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaussian}
 (P_tf)(x)=\int_{\Rd}q(t,x,y)f(y)\dd y,\qquad
 0\le q(t,x,y)\le \frac{C_T}{t^{d/2}}
 \exp\!\left(-\frac{|x-y|^2}{c_Tt}\right).
\end{equation}
The density identity first holds for nonnegative Borel $f$, then for signed $f$ when the integral of $|f|$ is finite. The estimate is a \emph{low-order analytic input}; it does not presuppose the interior smoothing theorem to be proved later.

For $\theta\ge0$ set $w_\theta(x)=e^{\theta|x|}$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Btheta}
 \mathcal B_\theta
 =\left\{f:\Rd\to\R\text{ Borel}:\norm f_\theta<\infty\right\},
 \qquad\norm f_\theta=\sup_{x\in\Rd}\frac{|f(x)|}{w_\theta(x)}.
\end{equation}
These are spaces of \emph{functions}, not equivalence classes modulo Lebesgue-null sets.

\begin{proposition}[Admissible exponential payoffs]\label{prop:weights}
Under $(\mathrm H_0)$, for each $\theta\ge0$ and $T>0$ there exists $M_{\theta,T}<\infty$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weightbound}
 P_tw_\theta(x)\le M_{\theta,T}w_\theta(x),\qquad
 \norm{P_tf}_\theta\le M_{\theta,T}\norm f_\theta,
 \qquad 0\le t\le T.
\end{equation}
The expectations in \eqref{eq:P} are absolutely finite for $f\in\mathcal B_\theta$, and the semigroup identity extends to every $\mathcal B_\theta$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
From $|y|\le|x|+|y-x|$ and \eqref{eq:gaussian}, change variables $z=(y-x)/\sqrt t$ to find
\[
 P_tw_\theta(x)\le C_Te^{\theta|x|}
 \int_{\Rd}e^{-|z|^2/c_T+\theta\sqrt T|z|}\dd z.
\]
The Gaussian integral is finite. The estimate for $f$ follows from positivity; conditioning and Tonelli first applied to $|f|$ prove the extended composition law. The assertion at $t=0$ is immediate.
\end{proof}

In particular, every polynomial-growth payoff lies in $\mathcal B_\theta$ for any $\theta>0$, while a vanilla payoff growing like $e^{x_i}$ lies in $\mathcal B_1$. The upper bound on the \emph{transition density} is essential here. Pointwise existence of $\E_x|f(X_t)|$ alone would not show that $P_t$ preserves a fixed weighted norm. Global Lipschitz coefficients without boundedness also do \emph{not} guarantee exponential moments: for $\dd X_t=X_t\dd W_t$ and $x>0$, $X_t$ is lognormal and $\E_xe^{\theta X_t}=\infty$ for $t,\theta>0$.

\subsubsection{Step II: a topology with strong continuity}
\label{subsec:continuity}
The algebraic identity $P_{t+s}=P_tP_s$ says nothing about convergence in a norm as $t\downarrow0$. Even for Brownian motion and $f=\ind_{\{0\}}$, $P_tf\equiv0$ for every $t>0$ while $f(0)=1$. Hence there is no strong continuity in the supremum norm on $B_b$, and similarly none on all of $\mathcal B_\theta$ with \eqref{eq:Btheta}.

There are several meaningful, but \emph{different}, choices of topology.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)},leftmargin=7mm,itemsep=5pt]
\item \textbf{The Feller space.} Under $(\mathrm H_0)$, $P_t$ preserves $C_0(\Rd)$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:C0}
 \lim_{t\downarrow0}\norm{P_tf-f}_\infty=0
 \qquad(f\in C_0(\Rd)).
\end{equation}
The proof combines continuity of the SDE in its initial state with uniform small-time control on compact sets and control of visits from spatial infinity. This is a strongly continuous, positive contraction semigroup; compare \cite[Ch.~4, \S 2]{ethier-kurtz}.

\item \textbf{Weighted continuous data.} Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:C0theta}
 C_{0,\theta}=\{f:\ f/w_\theta\in C_0(\Rd)\}
\end{equation}
with norm $\norm\cdot_\theta$. The Gaussian estimate and the Feller property imply $P_t(C_{0,\theta})\subset C_{0,\theta}$ and strong continuity there, by approximation with compactly supported continuous functions and \eqref{eq:weightbound}. A continuous function bounded by $Ce^{\theta|x|}$ belongs to $C_{0,\eta}$ whenever $\eta>\theta$. Discontinuous digitals need not belong to these spaces.

\item \textbf{Lebesgue space.} The kernel bound supplies a separate, strongly continuous semigroup on $L^2(\Rd)$, as proved next. The equality of an $L^2$ orbit with the pricing expectation is an almost-everywhere statement. Nonzero constants and ordinary exponential spot payoffs are not in global $L^2$.
\end{enumerate}
An invariant probability measure, when available, also gives contractive $L^p$ realizations by Jensen's inequality, but no such invariant measure is assumed here. Nor does sub-Markovian contractivity on $B_b$ imply $L^2(\dd x)$ contractivity: Lebesgue measure need not be invariant.

\begin{proposition}[Strong $L^2$ realization]\label{prop:L2}
Under $(\mathrm H_0)$, the pricing maps restricted to $L^2(\Rd)\cap B_b(\Rd)$ extend uniquely to a strongly continuous semigroup $(S_2(t))_{t\ge0}$ of bounded operators on $L^2(\Rd)$. For $0\le t\le T$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:L2bound}
 \norm{S_2(t)}_{L^2\to L^2}\le C_T.
\end{equation}
On bounded, square-integrable payoffs $S_2(t)f=P_tf$ almost everywhere.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Schur's test applies to \eqref{eq:gaussian}: $\sup_x\int q(t,x,y)\dd y\le1$ and $\sup_y\int q(t,x,y)\dd x\le C_T$. These bounds yield \eqref{eq:L2bound}. For $\phi\in C_c^\infty$, SDE stochastic continuity gives $P_t\phi\to\phi$ uniformly on compact sets, while Gaussian tails give an $L^2$-integrable dominating tail away from $\supp\phi$. Hence $\norm{P_t\phi-\phi}_2\to0$. Density of $C_c^\infty$ in $L^2$ and uniform boundedness for small $t$ give the assertion. Composition follows first for bounded functions and then by approximation.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Step III: the closed generator and its differential realizations}
\label{subsec:generator}
For smooth test functions, It\^o's formula identifies the \emph{formal} discounted Kolmogorov operator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A}
 L\phi=a^{ij}\partial_{ij}\phi+b^j\partial_j\phi,
 \qquad A\phi=L\phi-r\phi.
\end{equation}
Repeated spatial indices are summed from $1$ to $d$. Applied to the discounted process, It\^o's formula and Fubini give Dynkin's identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dynkin}
 P_t\phi-\phi=\int_0^tP_s(A\phi)\dd s,
 \qquad\phi\in C_c^\infty(\Rd).
\end{equation}
This alone does \emph{not} assert that $u(t,x)=P_t\phi(x)$ is $C^{1,2}$; classical spatial regularity requires further PDE input.

Let $G_2$ be the generator of $S_2$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G2}
 \dom(G_2)=\left\{g\in L^2:\lim_{t\downarrow0}
 \frac{S_2(t)g-g}{t}\text{ exists in }L^2\right\},\quad
 G_2g=\lim_{t\downarrow0}\frac{S_2(t)g-g}{t}.
\end{equation}
The operator $G_2$ is closed and densely defined. By \eqref{eq:dynkin} and strong $L^2$ continuity, $C_c^\infty\subset\dom(G_2)$ and $G_2\phi=A\phi$ on that class. This is an \emph{extension statement}, not yet a core theorem.

\begin{proposition}[Identification of the whole-space $L^2$ generator]\label{prop:H2}
Under $(\mathrm H_0)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H2}
 \boxed{\dom(G_2)=H^2(\Rd),\qquad G_2g=Ag,\qquad
 \norm g_2+\norm{G_2g}_2\asymp\norm g_{H^2}.}
\end{equation}
In particular, $C_c^\infty(\Rd)$ is a \emph{core} for $G_2$, meaning it is dense in $\dom(G_2)$ for the graph norm $\norm g_2+\norm{G_2g}_2$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch, with analytic input made explicit]
Uniform ellipticity, bounded Lipschitz $a$, and bounded lower-order terms give the global elliptic estimate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:elliptic-est}
 \norm g_{H^2}\le C\big(\norm{Ag}_2+\norm g_2\big)
 \qquad(g\in H^2(\Rd)),
\end{equation}
and for all sufficiently large real $\mu$, the map $\mu-A:H^2(\Rd)\to L^2(\Rd)$ is an isomorphism. These are standard whole-space $W^2_2$ results; see Krylov \cite[Ch.~1]{krylov} (the nondivergence formulation, using that $a$ is Lipschitz). The reverse graph estimate follows immediately from boundedness of the coefficients.

Since $C_c^\infty$ is dense in $H^2$ and $G_2$ is closed, \eqref{eq:dynkin} implies $A:H^2\to L^2$ is contained in $G_2$. Choose $\mu$ larger than the growth bound of $S_2$ and large enough for the elliptic resolvent result. Then $\mu-G_2$ and $\mu-A|_{H^2}$ are both onto $L^2$, and $\mu-G_2$ is injective. Their inclusion forces equality of the two operators, proving \eqref{eq:H2}. Density in $H^2$ now yields the asserted core property.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Unboundedness and abstract evolution]
The generator is not a bounded map $L^2\to L^2$. The \emph{continuous} extension is $A:H^2\to L^2$, or equivalently $G_2:(\dom G_2,\norm\cdot_{G_2})\to L^2$. For $f\in L^2$, $u(t)=S_2(t)f$ is the mild solution of $u'=G_2u$ and $u(0)=f$. If $f\in H^2$, it is a classical \emph{$L^2$-valued} solution. Neither phrase means automatically that $u$ is a classical $C^{1,2}$ solution in $(t,x)$; see \cite[Ch.~1, \S\S 1--3]{ethier-kurtz}.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{Step IV: a local weak realization and its initial trace}
\label{subsec:weak}
The preceding $L^2$ realization is useful but excludes many financial payoffs. Its role will be to construct \emph{compactly truncated} solutions; local energy bounds will then permit removal of the truncation without requiring $f\in L^2(\Rd)$.

Because $a$ is globally Lipschitz, $\partial_i a^{ij}$ exists almost everywhere and is bounded. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:beta}
 \beta^j=b^j-\partial_i a^{ij}.
\end{equation}
In the sense of distributions,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:divA}
 Av=\partial_i(a^{ij}\partial_jv)+\beta^j\partial_jv-rv.
\end{equation}
Let $B\subset\Rd$ be a bounded ball. For $v\in H^1(B)$ and $\varphi\in H_0^1(B)$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:AB}
 \inner{A_Bv}{\varphi}
 =-\int_B a^{ij}\partial_jv\,\partial_i\varphi\dd x
 +\int_B\beta^j\partial_jv\,\varphi\dd x
 -\int_Brv\varphi\dd x.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ABbound}
 A_B:H^1(B)\longrightarrow H^{-1}(B)=(H_0^1(B))^*,\qquad
 \norm{A_Bv}_{H^{-1}(B)}\le C_B\norm v_{H^1(B)}
\end{equation}
is a bounded \emph{weak differential realization}. It is \emph{not} being declared the generator of a boundary-value semigroup on $B$, and it imposes no boundary trace on $v$.

\begin{definition}[Local energy solution with payoff trace]\label{def:energy}
Let $\Omega\subset\Rd$ be open, $f\in L^2_{\loc}(\Omega)$, and $u:(0,T)\times\Omega\to\R$ be measurable. We say that $u$ solves $\partial_tu=Au$ locally with initial trace $f$ if, for every $B\Subset\Omega$ that is a ball, every $\chi\in C_c^\infty(B)$, and every $T'<T$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localclass}
 \chi u\in L^2(0,T';H_0^1(B)),\qquad
 \partial_t(\chi u)\in L^2(0,T';H^{-1}(B)),
\end{equation}
and $u$ satisfies
\begin{align}\label{eq:weakid}
 &-\int_0^{T'}\!\int_\Omega u\,\partial_t\varphi\dd x\dd t
 +\int_0^{T'}\!\int_\Omega a^{ij}\partial_ju\,\partial_i\varphi\dd x\dd t\notag\\
 &\quad-\int_0^{T'}\!\int_\Omega\beta^j\partial_ju\,\varphi\dd x\dd t
 +\int_0^{T'}\!\int_\Omega ru\varphi\dd x\dd t
 =\int_\Omega f(x)\varphi(0,x)\dd x
\end{align}
for all $\varphi\in C_c^\infty([0,T')\times\Omega)$. The integrals involving $\nabla u$ are interpreted locally on the support of $\varphi$. The Lions--Magenes Hilbert-triple lemma implies $\chi u\in C([0,T'];L^2(B))$; we require $(\chi u)(0)=\chi f$ in $L^2(B)$.
\end{definition}

The weak identity encodes the PDE and its trace but should not be confused with a statement that a pointwise Borel payoff is recovered pointwise at every initial state. For discontinuous $f$, convergence at $t=0$ is in local $L^2$; for $t>0$ the Gaussian kernel selects a continuous representative under the present hypotheses. For the time-continuity/energy framework see \cite[\S 7.1]{evans} and \cite[Ch.~III, \S\S 1--4]{ladyzhenskaya}.

\begin{theorem}[Expectation-to-energy bridge]\label{thm:bridge}
Assume $(\mathrm H_0)$. Let $f$ be a Borel payoff satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:payoffgrowth}
 |f(x)|\le K e^{\theta|x|}
 \qquad(x\in\Rd),\qquad K<\infty,\ \theta\ge0.
\end{equation}
Let $u(t,x)=P_tf(x)$ for $0\le t\le T$, with $u(0,x)=f(x)$. Then, on \emph{every} open $\Omega\subset\Rd$, $u$ is a local energy solution with payoff trace in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:energy}. In particular, for every $U\Subset\Rd$ and $T'<T$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bridgeconc}
 u\in C([0,T'];L^2(U))\cap L^2(0,T';H^1(U)),
 \qquad \partial_tu\in L^2(0,T';H^{-1}(U)),
\end{equation}
The $H^{-1}(U)$ derivative is understood by testing against $H_0^1(U)$. The initial trace is strong:
\[
  \lim_{t\downarrow0}\norm{u(t)-f}_{L^2(U)}=0.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof in four steps]
\textbf{1. The compact-payoff $L^2$ energy problem.}
Let $f_R=f\,\ind_{\{|x|\le R\}}$. Then $f_R\in L^2(\Rd)\cap L^\infty(\Rd)$, and $u_R(t)=S_2(t)f_R=P_tf_R$ a.e. on $\Rd$. Consider the bounded, uniformly coercive-up-to-a-shift bilinear form on $H^1(\Rd)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:form}
 \mathfrak a(v,\psi)=\int_{\Rd}a^{ij}\partial_jv\,\partial_i\psi
 -\int_{\Rd}\beta^j\partial_jv\,\psi
 +\int_{\Rd}rv\psi.
\end{equation}
It satisfies a G\r{a}rding inequality $\mathfrak a(v,v)+\omega\norm v_2^2\ge c\norm v_{H^1}^2$ for suitable $\omega,c>0$. Form evolution theory provides a unique
\begin{equation}\label{eq:globalenergy}
 v_R\in L^2(0,T;H^1(\Rd))\cap
 H^1(0,T;H^{-1}(\Rd))\cap C([0,T];L^2(\Rd))
\end{equation}
with $v_R(0)=f_R$ and $\partial_tv_R=Av_R$ in $H^{-1}$. The corresponding form semigroup agrees with $S_2$: both generators extend $A$ on $C_c^\infty$, and the $H^2$ resolvent identification in Proposition~\ref{prop:H2} makes the realization unique. Hence $v_R=u_R$. For form semigroups and weak Cauchy problems see \cite[Chs.~1, 4]{ouhabaz} and \cite[\S 7.1]{evans}. This step avoids assuming differentiability of $P_tf_R$.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{2. A uniform local price bound and payoff truncation.}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:weights}, for every bounded $V\subset\Rd$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localL2bound}
 \sup_{R>0}\ \sup_{0\le t\le T}\norm{u_R(t)}_{L^2(V)}
 \le K M_{\theta,T}\norm{w_\theta}_{L^2(V)}<\infty.
\end{equation}
For every $M>0$, the Gaussian bound moreover gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tail}
 \lim_{R\to\infty}\ 
 \sup_{\substack{0\le t\le T\\ |x|\le M}}
 |P_t(f-f_R)(x)|=0.
\end{equation}
Indeed, in \eqref{eq:gaussian} use $|y|\le M+|y-x|$ and complete the square in $-{|y-x|^2}/(c_Tt)+\theta|y-x|$. A Gaussian tail outside $|y-x|>R-M$, uniformly for $0<t\le T$, tends to zero. At $t=0$, $f_R=f$ on $B_M$ once $R>M$. Thus $u_R\to u$ uniformly on $[0,T]\times B_M$ \emph{as a truncation statement}, regardless of whether $f$ is continuous. In particular the convergence is strong in $L^2((0,T)\times V)$ for every bounded $V$.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{3. Uniform Caccioppoli estimate down to the initial time.}
Fix concentric (or simply nested) balls $U\Subset V$ and choose $\eta\in C_c^\infty(V)$ with $\eta=1$ on $U$. Test the $H^{-1}$ evolution for $u_R$ against $\eta^2u_R$ (justified by time regularization and \eqref{eq:globalenergy}). Ellipticity, boundedness of $a,\beta,r$, and Young's inequality yield
\begin{equation}\label{eq:caccioppoli}
 \sup_{0\le s\le T'}\int_U|u_R(s)|^2\dd x
 +\int_0^{T'}\!\int_U|\nabla u_R|^2\dd x\dd t
 \le C_{U,V,T'}\!\left(
 \int_V|f_R|^2\dd x+
 \int_0^{T'}\!\int_V|u_R|^2\dd x\dd t\right).
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:localL2bound} the right-hand side is independent of $R$. This estimate starts at $t=0$ and is crucial: a pointwise smoothing bound $|\nabla u(t)|\lesssim t^{-1/2}$ cannot simply be squared and integrated at zero. The estimate is a standard local form of the energy inequality in \cite[\S 7.1]{evans} and \cite[Ch.~III, \S 2]{ladyzhenskaya}.

Using \eqref{eq:divA}, the same bounds show, for each $\chi\in C_c^\infty(U)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:timebound}
 \sup_R\norm{\partial_t(\chi u_R)}_{L^2(0,T';H^{-1}(U))}<\infty.
\end{equation}
The product $\chi u_R$ has zero trace on $\partial U$; commutator terms involving $\nabla\chi$ are bounded by the local $L^2$ and $H^1$ estimates. They are \emph{not} an imposed Dirichlet condition on $u_R$.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{4. Weak compactness, PDE identity, and strong trace.}
For each pair $U\Subset V$, \eqref{eq:caccioppoli} bounds $u_R$ in $L^2(0,T';H^1(U))$. Along a subsequence its gradients converge weakly in $L^2((0,T')\times U)$. Since $u_R\to u$ strongly in local $L^2$, the weak gradient limit is $\nabla u$. Equation \eqref{eq:timebound} gives the corresponding local $H^{-1}$ derivative. The coefficients are fixed as $R\to\infty$, so passing to the limit in the weak formulation of the $u_R$ equation yields \eqref{eq:weakid} for $u$, including the initial term: $f_R=f$ on $\supp\varphi(0,\cdot)$ for large $R$.

Finally, each cutoff $\chi u_R$ is continuous $[0,T']\to L^2(U)$ by \eqref{eq:globalenergy}. The uniform-on-compact estimate \eqref{eq:tail} gives $\chi u_R\to\chi u$ in $C([0,T'];L^2(U))$, so the limit is continuous there with $(\chi u)(0)=\chi f$. Taking a cutoff equal to one on an arbitrary smaller set proves the asserted local strong trace. A covering argument establishes the result on every open $\Omega$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Precisely what has, and has not, been proved]
The theorem identifies the \emph{selected pricing expectation} with a weak parabolic solution having locally square-integrable gradients, an $H^{-1}$ time derivative, and a strong local $L^2$ initial trace. It does \emph{not} assert uniqueness among all local weak solutions on an unbounded domain without growth restrictions. Nor does it impose a spatial boundary condition or claim that every pointwise-finite expectation has these properties. Exponential growth is one sufficient global admissibility condition, not the largest possible class.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{Boundary realizations and subsequent regularity theory}
\label{subsec:boundary}
The diffusion in \eqref{eq:SDE} lives on all of $\Rd$. If instead a process is killed on leaving a domain, reflected, or given boundary cash flows, the \emph{global} semigroup and the domain of its generator change. The local bilinear operator $A_B$ in \eqref{eq:AB} is deliberately not a boundary generator; it describes the same interior differential expression after spatial localization. In particular, multiplying by a cutoff produces a commutator $[A,\chi]u=A(\chi u)-\chi Au$: the cutoff problem is not a homogeneous Dirichlet pricing problem.

Once the local energy property is established, interior parabolic regularity can be applied on cylinders strictly inside $(0,T)\times\Omega$. Divergence-form energy and H\"older estimates are treated in \cite[Ch.~III]{ladyzhenskaya}; nondivergence-form Sobolev estimates in \cite[Chs.~2, 4--5]{krylov}; probabilistic motivations and further elliptic/parabolic smoothing in \cite{bass,stroock}. Under additional coefficient regularity, one may obtain $W^{1,2}_p$ or $C^{1+\alpha/2,2+\alpha}$ regularity for positive time, but \emph{none of those higher-order conclusions was used to prove Theorem~\ref{thm:bridge}}.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Conclusion of the bridge.} The whole-space diffusion and an admissible payoff determine $P_tf$. Gaussian bounds first enlarge the payoff class and provide an $L^2$ realization on truncated data. The closed $H^2\to L^2$ generator explains the abstract evolution, while the separate $H^1\to H^{-1}$ realization and local Caccioppoli estimates extend the PDE to exponential-growth Borel payoffs. The resulting strong local $L^2$ trace and energy regularity are exactly the hypotheses needed to start an \emph{independent} interior regularity argument. This preserves the distinction between the global pricing rule and the local PDE mechanism.
